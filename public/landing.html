Why this exists

Built by a guy who needed it

I train Jiu Jitsu a few mornings a week. I lift. I used to ride long distance up the coast. And somewhere in my fifties I noticed that every app, every program, every influencer plan was written for someone half my age: crash-cut calories, grind to failure, no thought given to joints, sleep, hormones, or the fact that recovery is different now.

There was nothing that addressed what a man in midlife actually needs: nutrition that protects muscle while the fat comes off, supplements chosen for joints and recovery instead of pump, and training that respects the body you have while still asking something of it.

So I built Protocol. It starts where you are: your age, your goal, the activities you already love, the joints that complain. It builds your week around your real life, tells you what to eat in portions you measure with your hand, and then it listens every Sunday and adjusts. No shame, no leaderboards, no 22-year-old yelling at you.

What it does

A plan that respects the decades

Starts where you are.

Your age, goal, experience, equipment, and the activities you already do, from Jiu Jitsu to hot yoga. Tell it you train at 6am Tuesdays and your meals adjust around it.

Eat with your hands.

No scale, no weighing, no apps full of guilt. Palms of protein, fists of vegetables, cupped hands of carbs that follow your training days. It works at any restaurant.

Recovery is the program.

Deloads tuned to your age. Exercise swaps for cranky joints. A supplement stack built for joints and sleep, not just biceps. The last grinding rep costs more than it pays, so we do not take it.

It listens every Sunday.

A two-minute check-in reshapes next week automatically: push when you are fresh, back off when you are not. Log your food and lifts if you want, and it gets even smarter.

Your plan lands every morning at 6am, on Slack or email, with a how-to video for every exercise. Like a coach who actually pays attention.
The company you keep

Like-minded men, same chapter

This is not a high-octane tracker chasing streaks and badges. It is a growing group of men in their forties, fifties, and beyond who decided the second half gets trained for on purpose: fathers, professionals, weekend grapplers, former athletes easing back in. Nobody here is trying to look 25. We are trying to be strong at 75.
